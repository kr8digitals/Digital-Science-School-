## Page: /resources/study-plan

Study skills · 4 MIN READ

### A study plan you can actually stick to

Small, focused sessions. Clear goals. A little space to breathe.

### Start with one specific goal

“Study chemistry” is vague. “Explain the difference between an element and a compound” gives you a finish line. Choose one goal for each session.

### Try a simple weekly rhythm

Monday: revisit a difficult topic. Tuesday: practise questions. Wednesday: review mistakes. Thursday: explain the topic aloud. Friday: take a short self-test. Keep sessions manageable and add breaks.

### Use recall, not just rereading

Close your notes and write what you remember. Check the gaps, then try again. Recalling information helps you find what still needs work.

### Leave room to adjust

If a topic takes longer than expected, move the plan—not your confidence. Share persistent difficulties with a teacher or mentor.

## Page: /resources/fractions

Mathematics · 3 MIN READ

### Fractions, without the frustration

Learn why common denominators make adding fractions easier.

### A fraction is a part of a whole

In 3/4, the denominator 4 tells us the whole has four equal parts. The numerator 3 tells us we have three of those parts.

### Make the pieces the same size

To add 1/2 and 1/4, first write 1/2 as 2/4. Now both fractions describe quarters, so 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

### Try it yourself

What is 1/3 + 1/6? Rewrite 1/3 as 2/6. Add the numerators: 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6. Simplify to 1/2.

### A useful check

Adding two positive fractions should give a larger value than either fraction alone. Draw a rectangle divided into equal parts if the answer feels uncertain.

## Page: /resources/science-everyday

Science · 4 MIN READ

### Think like a scientist. Start at home.

A simple, safe observation activity to build scientific thinking.

### Begin with a question

Does a paper towel absorb more water than ordinary writing paper? Turn a everyday observation into a question you can investigate.

### Make a fair comparison

With an adult nearby, place equal-sized pieces of paper on a washable tray. Add the same small amount of water to each. Keep paper size and water amount the same.

### Observe and record

Write down what you see after the same amount of time. Repeat the comparison. Use observations, not guesses, to explain the result. Keep water away from electrical devices.

### Think beyond the result

What else could affect absorption? Paper thickness, texture or the amount of water? A useful investigation often leads to another good question.

## Page: /resources/exam-strategy

Exam preparation · 5 MIN READ

### Your next mock exam is a map, not a verdict

Turn the questions you miss into a more useful revision plan.

### Sort your mistakes

Put each missed question into one of three groups: a concept you did not understand, a reading or calculation error, or a time-management problem. Different mistakes need different fixes.

### Relearn before repeating

If the concept is unclear, revisit it with a teacher or trusted textbook. Doing ten more questions without understanding the method may reinforce the same mistake.

### Practise with purpose

Try a few questions on the weakest topic. Explain each step, then complete a short timed set. Compare your reasoning with the answer explanation.

### Keep perspective

One practice score is a snapshot, not a prediction. Look for patterns over several attempts, and ask your mentor for help choosing your priorities. These are original study tips, not official examination-board guidance.

## Page: /resources/coding-first

Digital skills · 3 MIN READ

### Your first algorithm needs no computer

Discover the thinking behind code with an everyday task.

### An algorithm is a set of steps

Think about packing your school bag. You check the timetable, find each book, put it in the bag and check that nothing is missing. That is a sequence of instructions.

### Be precise

“Get ready” is too broad for a computer. Break it into small, clear actions. What happens first? Which action depends on another?

### Add a decision

If tomorrow includes mathematics, pack your mathematics book. Otherwise, skip it. This is the idea behind a conditional statement.

### Try a challenge

Write instructions for drawing a square. Ask someone to follow them exactly. If the result differs from your plan, improve the instructions. That is the beginning of debugging.

This is a draft operational notice for AFRISTEM Digital Science School. It must be reviewed and approved by the school before public launch. In development preview, use fictional details only. Contact info@afristemschools.org about privacy questions.

Information is used to assess applications, respond to enquiries and organise admissions or recruitment. Authorised admissions staff can review applications and documents. An essential staff session cookie expires after eight hours; inactivity expires access after 30 minutes. Named staff access, document downloads and important account changes are logged for accountability. Transactional email notices and SMTP acceptance records are stored in a private queue; SMTP acceptance does not guarantee inbox delivery. Public visitors do not need an account or tracking cookie. The school must set and publish approved retention periods before launch; this build has no automatic record-deletion schedule.

### Access, corrections and concerns

Contact info@afristemschools.org to request access, correction or deletion, or to raise a privacy concern. The school may need to verify your identity and consider applicable legal obligations. A production privacy review must address Nigeria’s data protection requirements and any applicable international obligations.

SAFE TO ASK. SAFE TO LEARN.

### Child safeguarding approach

Transparent information for learners, families and educators.

### A foundation, not a box to tick

This draft sets out the safeguards AFRISTEM should operationalise before teaching begins. The school must approve the policy, appoint a safeguarding lead, verify reporting contacts and train staff before launch.

### Respectful learning spaces

No bullying, harassment, humiliating feedback, sexualised conduct, discrimination or threats should be tolerated. Lessons and feedback should be age-appropriate and respectful. Learners should know they can speak up without being blamed.

### Teacher–learner communication

Use school-approved channels with appropriate guardian visibility. Do not ask learners to move to private or secret conversations, share personal photographs or disclose unnecessary private information. One-to-one arrangements must follow the school’s approved supervision rules.

### Safer recruitment

Verify identity, qualifications, references and applicable background checks before appointments. Require a code of conduct, safeguarding training and a clear procedure for responding to concerns. Submitted documents alone are not proof that checks have been completed.

### Recording and media consent

Explain whether lessons will be recorded, who can access recordings and how long they are kept. Obtain appropriate guardian permission and learner assent for photography, recordings or promotional use. Do not treat application consent as permission to use a child’s image.

### Raise a concern

A learner should tell a trusted adult if something feels wrong. Contact the school at info@afristemschools.org or +234 810 554 1242 and state that the matter concerns safeguarding. These are the supplied general school contacts, not a verified emergency service. In immediate danger, seek help from local emergency services. The school must publish an independent escalation route before launch.

### Responsible handling

Share concerns only with authorised people who need the information to protect the learner. Record facts accurately, preserve relevant evidence safely, and do not promise absolute secrecy or investigate allegations informally. Follow applicable reporting duties and specialist guidance.
